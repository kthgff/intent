# Product deep review — 2026-10-04

## 1. Verdict

**Intent addresses a real problem, but the current design promises more certainty than its evidence model can establish.** Its useful core is not a canvas, a ticket hierarchy, or an autonomous coding pipeline: it is the accountable chain from a human decision to a specific implementation and an observed result. D26–D28 added the missing front door and closing surfaces, but several fixes substitute a label for a guarantee: a reachable app becomes `released`, amended provenance becomes historical truth, and an approved ancestor becomes trusted downstream context without a complete invalidation rule. Meanwhile, the product is approaching a full planning suite before demonstrating that it saves its operator attention. Keep the idea-to-production ambition and Jira-familiar interactions; narrow the first supported journey and make every approval, recovery action, and completion claim defensible.

**Scope and method.** Read the current product contracts in the requested order, the prior reviews, decision log, screen inventory and relevant screen designs; inspected representative UI, API, conductor, storage, KB, prompt, and test code. This is a static product/process review, not a live usability study, runtime security audit, or fresh test run. No application code, contracts, or prior reviews were changed. Suggestions here are **proposed**, not ratified decisions or implementation work.

Evidence labels used below:

- **Observed:** directly present in the inspected code or document.
- **Target:** ratified design whose implementation is still pending.
- **Inference:** a failure scenario or product hypothesis derived from that design; not a reproduced incident or customer-research result.
- **Aspirational:** a capability claim without a sufficiently defined end-to-end implementation contract in the reviewed material. This does not mean it is infeasible.

Historical reviews are context, not current truth. In particular, S-18, scoped synthesis, capture/tray, owner/priority fields, re-plan, feedback, and verification are now designed. This review does **not** rediscover them as missing. It tests whether their proposed semantics work.

## 2. Concept assessment

### The promise, sharpened

> Turn rough intent into a reviewable, released change—with explicit human decisions and evidence connecting what was requested, approved, built, deployed, and observed.

Use “help prove which requirements were checked” rather than an unqualified “the machine proves the spec held.” Coverage lint, a different review model, and a passing command are different kinds of evidence, not interchangeable proof of correctness. The PRD already acknowledges that a coverage matrix proves a case name exists, not that the test is real; that honesty should govern the whole product. [PRD.md](PRD.md), §§2 and 6, “Document pipeline”; [SCHEMAS.md](SCHEMAS.md), §§4 and 10.

Separate three outcomes in the UI:

1. **Implemented:** a particular code revision passed stated checks.
2. **Released:** that revision was observed in the intended environment and audience, with the required verification evidence.
3. **Validated:** the change helped the intended user or business outcome—or evidence showed it did not.

Not every change needs a business experiment. Every completion claim does need a defined meaning. A release may be successful even when its hypothesis fails; the history should record both facts.

### Who it is for—and who it is not

**Recommended first user:** a technical founder or senior engineer responsible for one product, already using coding agents, who can judge architecture and delivery risk but needs help maintaining context and reviewing asynchronous work. This is a recommendation grounded in the local CLI/worktree architecture and current single configured actor, not a validated market segment. [PRD.md](PRD.md), §§2–3; [packages/api/src/server.ts](../packages/api/src/server.ts), `createServer`, L177–182.

**Next user:** a small-team technical lead, after authenticated identity, execution ownership, shared artifacts, and collaboration recovery work end to end. A Postgres connection and `owner` chip alone do not make the solo engine a team product.

**Do not initially promise:** a nontechnical founder can describe any application and safely operate it in production; enterprise compliance; a hosted always-available capture service; or a general replacement for every Jira workflow. None is established by the current delivery and permission model. [PRD.md](PRD.md), §2 delivery architecture; [MVP.md](MVP.md), deferred roles and daemon delivery; [API.md](API.md), Auth.

**Buyer hypothesis:** the technical owner pays for less rework and a decision record that survives personnel/model changes, not for another place to arrange cards. Test that proposition before making collaboration seats, storage, or inference markup the commercial model. Pricing and willingness to pay remain unvalidated; [PRODUCT-MISSION.md](PRODUCT-MISSION.md), “Open questions,” already identifies this uncertainty.

### The moat, stated plainly

The potentially defensible asset is a **trusted, longitudinal decision-and-outcome record**: which requirement and source version produced which decision, what independent checks established, what actually ran, what failed, and what the next attempt learned. Useful project-specific history and reliable recovery can compound. The entity names, canvas, prompt templates, and stage runner are reproducible features; no competitive exclusivity is established by this repository.

One strength worth preserving: the shared Next-action operational hub makes human judgment a first-class part of execution rather than an afterthought. [design/s-05-ticket-focus.md](design/s-05-ticket-focus.md), “Information hierarchy.”

### Simplest true version versus the current design

The smallest credible journey is:

`capture → select an outcome → approve a bounded plan → implement one vertical slice → inspect the working result and code → record deployment evidence → review the outcome`.

For the first pilot, support one technical operator, one local engine, an existing repo, text/link capture, an optional simple board, one feature-sized objective, child tickets when necessary, and one externally managed deployment target. Keep the web UI; do not regress to a CLI-only product. Do not require an Initiative wrapper for a small Feature or a canvas exercise for a direct Ticket. Existing orphan-ticket and manual-creation decisions support this direction. [FEATURES.md](FEATURES.md), §§1 and 3.

Treat Boards as **views and source collections**, not compulsory ancestors. Projects own work; Initiatives coordinate several Features when useful; Features express deliverable outcomes; Tickets partition implementation. The large model may remain available without imposing all its levels on every task.

The current scope adds multimedia canvas editing, semantic retrieval, two decomposition layers, multi-install coordination, mobile/webhook capture, many approval variants, and 20 inventory pages. These may be valuable later, but page coverage is not proof that the shortest journey works. [UI-INVENTORY.md](UI-INVENTORY.md), §§1–3; [PRD.md](PRD.md), §12 item 33.

### Counterexamples the revised design must pass

These are **proposed acceptance scenarios**, not tests run during this review.

1. **Healthy old deployment:** merge build B while environment still serves A. A green health page must not release B. The hub shows deployment evidence missing. F01.
2. **Approval race:** two reviewers load package v4; an agent writes v5 before one approves. Approval of v4 must neither approve v5 nor silently fail without explaining the change. Repeat after a PR fix changes the head SHA. F02.
3. **Shared-source discard:** one note contributes to two initiatives; one is partly released and then discarded. Historical provenance and release evidence survive; active consumption by the other initiative remains. F03/F08.
4. **Synthesis restart:** extraction completes for four of five attachments, the app restarts, and the user retries. No input is silently lost, no duplicate initiative is published, and the failed item stays visible. F04.
5. **Greenfield start:** a founder has notes but no repo, tests or hosted app. The system can explain and execute a bounded bootstrap plan—or honestly stop at “existing repo required,” rather than falsely passing preflight. F05.
6. **Deferred consequential question:** the sixteenth question concerns destructive data retention. It remains blocking or explicitly risk-dispositioned; it does not become consent because the question budget was exhausted. F09.
7. **Phone offline:** Save succeeds to durable local storage while the paired engine is unreachable. Copy says exactly that; reconnection creates one server item, not zero or two. F12.
8. **Dependency transition:** A moves through merged to released before B is claimed; B's code dependency remains satisfied. If A is later reverted, B is impact-assessed rather than silently considered safe. F08/F13.
9. **Lost machine:** restore on a clean host produces readable documents, source history and referenced evidence; old deployment/PR operations are reconciled, never blindly replayed. F14.
10. **Unknown test evidence:** a passing suite with no per-case result reports “suite passed; case verification unavailable,” not “all acceptance criteria verified.” F16.

### SaaS/product implications

- **Activation should mean a trusted useful decision or completed slice, not “settings filled in.”** Track where users stop before that point. The seeded board is a tutorial, not evidence of product value. Sources: WF-01, GAP-SOLUTIONS G11, F05/F18.
- **Retention should come from reusable context and less rework, not accumulating unprocessed cards.** Test whether prior decisions reduce repeated questions on the next change. Sources: FEATURES §3 prior-art memory; F10/F17.
- **The commercial boundary should follow the supportable trust model.** A single-machine product, a shared team runner and a hosted capture relay have different operating costs and privacy expectations. Validate which problem users will pay to solve before treating every channel as included. Sources: PRD §2, PRODUCT-MISSION Open questions; F12/F13/F15.
- **Portability is a trust feature, not an anti-business feature.** If the record is the value, demonstrate that customers can recover and export it. F14.

### Proposed pilot and design-review gates

Start with the already-proposed small real-ticket pilot, but include three **different situations**: a direct low-risk ticket, a feature split across dependent work, and an intentionally failed/revised attempt. If greenfield is in the promise, add an empty-repo case. These are learning cases, not a statistically credible market study. [MVP.md](MVP.md), Pilot plan; [PRD.md](PRD.md), §13 prompt-eval research.

Before expanding scope, answer with observed evidence:

- Can the operator tell what is approved, what is running, and what evidence is missing without opening several pages?
- Are fewer active human interventions required than in their existing agent workflow, without silently accepting more risk?
- Does a changed requirement invalidate the right downstream work and preserve unaffected approvals?
- Can an interrupted/failed attempt be resumed without duplicate work, duplicate external effects or lost ideas?
- Can the operator distinguish suite success, feature acceptance, deployment and validated user value?
- Can the full decision record survive a restore and be understood outside Intent?

**Subtraction recommendations:** retain optional hierarchy, a simple capture/list route, the existing operational hubs and one shared Kanban. Defer hosted email relay, elaborate media/canvas tooling, broad tracker parity and enterprise roles until the basic contract works. Remove mandatory hygiene gates, “Done means cancel” gestures, and blanket claims of mathematical proof. Do not add a new top-level screen for every recommendation in this report.

## 8. Open questions for the founder

These require product decisions; documentation or agents cannot resolve them by assertion.

1. **First customer:** a technical solo owner with an existing repo, or a founder starting a new app? **Recommendation:** existing-repo technical operator first; keep a separately validated greenfield milestone if the broader promise is central. F05/F18.
2. **Meaning of production:** must Intent deploy, or can it observe and attest a deployment performed by existing tools? **Recommendation:** external deployment evidence first, with build/environment identity and honest manual attestations. F01.
3. **Authority boundary:** who may approve spending, change requirements, override evidence and confirm release? **Recommendation:** explicit owner in solo mode; authenticated reviewer/operator separation before shared mode. F02/F13.
4. **Hierarchy:** must every Feature belong to an Initiative, and must every Initiative originate on a Board? **Recommendation:** no mandatory Board parent; Initiative grouping optional for small work, with one clearly specified orphan-Feature path. F08.
5. **What is immutable?** Are source history and release snapshots retained even when work is cancelled or provenance corrected? **Recommendation:** yes, with a separate intentional sensitive-content redaction policy—not deletion disguised as undo. F03/F15.
6. **Local-first reach:** are you willing to operate a hosted capture relay, or should capture initially require the user's device/paired engine? **Recommendation:** explicit pairing plus local offline drafts first; do not imply anywhere availability before choosing its trust and operating model. F12.
7. **User judgment:** can the product record “I don't know” and stop, rather than always presenting a recommended answer? **Recommendation:** yes; defaults reduce effort only for decisions whose risk is understood. F09.
8. **Business outcome:** should an Initiative record success criteria and a check-back result, or only engineering completion? **Recommendation:** lightweight optional outcome tracking, mandatory clarity about which claim is being made. F06/F16.
9. **Scope discipline:** will observed pilot value become a gate before expanding to every planned screen and integration? **Recommendation:** yes; ratified direction is not a requirement to implement every idea in the first release. F18.
10. **Commercial model:** is the paid value local productivity, team governance, or managed convenience? **Recommendation:** validate the buyer's willingness to pay for reduced rework and trustworthy history before coupling the roadmap to seats, relay hosting or inference resale. §2 audience/moat; F12/F13/F18.

*If a competitor cloned this spec tomorrow, they would still be missing validated operating judgment, trustworthy customer-specific decision/outcome history, and reliable recovery earned through real use—but this repository does not yet establish those as a durable moat.*
